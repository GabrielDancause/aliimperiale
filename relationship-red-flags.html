Understanding Red Flags

Healthy vs. Unhealthy Dynamics

Look, no relationship is perfect. We all have bad days, say things we shouldn't, or get a little defensive sometimes. But a healthy relationship is built on a foundation of respect, trust, and open communication. When you bring up an issue, your partner should be willing to listen—even if they disagree.

Unhealthy dynamics (often called "red flags") show up as patterns of behavior. It's not usually just one bad fight; it's a consistent disregard for your boundaries, feelings, or autonomy. Things like excessive jealousy, control over who you see or what you spend, and frequent emotional manipulation are warning signs that shouldn't be ignored.

Self-Reflection & Journaling Prompts

If your results left you feeling uncertain, or if you just want to dig a little deeper, grab a journal or open a note on your phone. Take a moment, free of judgment, to reflect on these questions:

  • When I am with my partner, do I feel more like myself or less like myself?
  • Do I feel safe expressing my honest feelings, even when they are negative?
  • How does my partner handle it when I set a boundary or say "no"?
  • Am I compromising my core values or sacrificing my independence for this relationship?

Emergency & Support Resources

If you felt unsafe while taking this assessment, or if you recognized patterns of abuse (physical, emotional, or financial), please know that you are not alone and help is available. This tool is for educational purposes and is not a substitute for professional help.

  • US National Domestic Violence Hotline: Call 1-800-799-SAFE (7233) or text "START" to 88788. thehotline.org
  • Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 to connect with a crisis counselor 24/7.
  • Canada Assaulted Women’s Helpline: Call 1-866-863-0511 or text #SAFE (#7233).
  • UK National Domestic Abuse Helpline: Call 0808 2000 247.

If you are in immediate danger, please call your local emergency services (911 in the US/Canada).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are common relationship red flags?
Common red flags include controlling behavior, constant criticism, lack of communication, disrespect for boundaries, isolation from friends/family, and inconsistent or untrustworthy actions.
How do I know if my relationship is toxic?
A toxic relationship consistently drains you rather than supports you. Signs include frequent intense arguments, feeling like you're 'walking on eggshells,' feeling manipulated, or your self-esteem dropping since the relationship began.
Can red flags be fixed?
Some 'yellow flags' or mild issues can be addressed through open communication and active effort from both partners (like couples therapy). However, severe red flags involving abuse, deep manipulation, or core incompatibility usually mean the relationship is unhealthy and potentially unsafe.
What is gaslighting in a relationship?
Gaslighting is a form of psychological manipulation where a person makes you doubt your own memories, perception, or sanity. They might deny things that happened, minimize your feelings, or shift the blame entirely onto you.
When should I leave a relationship?
You should consider leaving if there is any form of physical, emotional, or verbal abuse. Other reasons include persistent unhappiness, core values clashing, your boundaries repeatedly being ignored, or if the relationship negatively impacts your mental health.
