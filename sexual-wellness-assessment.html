Understanding Sexual Wellness

Sexual wellness is about more than just avoiding disease or unplanned pregnancy. It's a holistic, positive approach to sexuality and intimacy. It encompasses your physical ability to enjoy sex, your emotional connection to your body and your partners, and the knowledge required to navigate your desires safely and consensually.

Regularly checking in with yourself through tools like this assessment can help you identify areas where you might want to learn more, try something new, or seek professional support.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a sexual wellness assessment?
A sexual wellness assessment is a self-reflective tool designed to help you evaluate various aspects of your sexual health, including physical comfort, emotional state, communication skills, and overall relationship satisfaction. It serves as a starting point to identify areas for growth or support.
Why is sexual health important for overall well-being?
Sexual health is deeply interconnected with our physical, mental, and emotional health. A fulfilling sex life and positive relationship with your body can lower stress, improve sleep, boost immune function, and enhance emotional intimacy with partners.
When should I see a sex therapist?
You might consider seeing a sex therapist if you are experiencing persistent distress regarding intimacy, such as ongoing mismatched desire with a partner, difficulty communicating boundaries, anxiety surrounding sex, or physical discomfort that doesn't have a clear medical cause.
How can I improve communication about sex with my partner?
Start by having conversations outside of the bedroom when both of you are relaxed. Use "I" statements to express your feelings and desires rather than focusing on what your partner isn't doing. Tools like the "Yes/No/Maybe" list or a boundaries builder can provide a structured, low-pressure way to start the dialogue.
What are the dimensions of sexual wellness?
Common dimensions include physical health (function and safety), emotional well-being (feelings and self-esteem), cognitive knowledge (understanding anatomy and consent), relational intimacy (communication and connection), and socio-cultural factors (how your environment influences your sexuality).
