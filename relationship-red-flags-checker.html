Frequently Asked Questions About Red Flags

What is considered a red flag in a relationship?

A red flag in a relationship is a warning sign of unhealthy, toxic, or abusive behavior. This can include crossing boundaries, ignoring consent, trying to control your finances or social life, extreme jealousy, or verbal and emotional abuse.

How can I tell the difference between a minor issue and a red flag?

Minor issues are usually situational disagreements where both partners can communicate respectfully and find a compromise. Red flags are patterns of behavior that involve disrespect, manipulation, lack of accountability, or making you feel unsafe or less than.

What should I do if my partner exhibits controlling behavior?

Controlling behavior is a serious red flag. If it is safe to do so, try setting clear boundaries. However, if the behavior escalates or you feel threatened, do not try to fix them. Seek support from trusted friends, family, or professional domestic abuse organizations.

Is financial abuse a real thing?

Yes. Financial abuse occurs when one partner tries to control the other's access to money, prevents them from working, forces them to take on debt, or hides financial resources. It is a tactic used to trap someone in a relationship.

Where can I get help if I feel unsafe in my relationship?

If you feel unsafe, trust your instincts. Reach out to local domestic violence resources. In the US, you can contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-SAFE (7233) or text 'START' to 88788. You do not have to navigate this alone.
