Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle & Libido

Our bodies are beautifully complex, and it's totally normal for sexual desire to fluctuate throughout the month. When we track these changes, we can stop wondering "what's wrong with me?" on low-energy days and instead lean into what our bodies actually need.

Hormones and Desire

Estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone are the main hormones driving your cycle. During the follicular and ovulatory phases, estrogen and testosterone rise, often leading to increased natural lubrication, energy, and sexual desire. In the luteal and menstrual phases, progesterone dominates and estrogen drops, which can make you feel more inward, sleepy, or sensitive.

Partner Communication

I always say, communication is the ultimate foreplay! Sharing your cycle insights with a partner can take the pressure off. You can say things like, "I'm in my luteal phase right now, so I'm craving more cuddling and low-pressure touch," or "I'm ovulating and feeling super energized—let's try that new thing we talked about."

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my period sexual desire so low sometimes?

It's completely normal for period sexual desire to drop. During menstruation, your estrogen and progesterone levels are at their lowest. You might also be experiencing cramping or fatigue. Give yourself grace and focus on comforting touch rather than penetrative sex if that's what your body wants.

How does this hormone cycle calculator work?

This hormone cycle calculator estimates your current phase based on standard cycle algorithms (your cycle length and period length). It breaks down your cycle into four phases—menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, and luteal—to help you understand typical hormonal shifts and their impact on your sexuality and energy.

Does menstrual cycle libido change if I'm on birth control?

Yes! Hormonal birth control overrides your natural hormonal fluctuations to prevent ovulation. This means you might not experience the typical peaks and valleys in menstrual cycle libido described here. However, tracking your mood and energy can still provide valuable insights into your body's unique patterns.
