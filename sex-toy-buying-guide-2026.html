The Complete Sex Toy Buying Guide 2026

Your honest, judgment-free roadmap to finding the right toys for your body, budget, and pleasure.

Welcome to the Guide

Hey there! If you're reading this, you're probably curious about sex toys. Maybe you're looking to buy your very first one, or maybe you want to upgrade your collection with something a little more adventurous. Whatever your reason, you're in the right place.

The sex toy industry has changed a lot over the last few years. We're talking better materials, quieter motors, smarter designs, and a massive shift towards wellness and education. But with all these options, it can feel overwhelming.

That's exactly why I put together this guide. No shame, no judgment—just honest, practical advice to help you find what feels good for your body, your budget, and your relationships.

1. The Non-Negotiable: Material Safety

Let's start with the most important rule: what goes in or on your body needs to be safe. Not all toys are created equal, and unfortunately, the industry isn't fully regulated.

The Gold Standard (Body-Safe)

  • 100% Medical-Grade Silicone: The best of the best. It's non-porous (won't harbor bacteria), hypoallergenic, warms to your body temperature, and is incredibly easy to clean.
  • Borosilicate Glass: Hard, smooth, temperature-responsive (you can warm it in water or cool it in the fridge), and completely non-porous. Great for dildos and anal toys.
  • Stainless Steel / Metal: Heavy, smooth, and excellent for temperature play. Very easy to sterilize.
  • ABS Plastic: Hard plastic often used for the base or handle of toys (and sometimes bullet vibrators). It's non-porous and safe, though it doesn't have the soft feel of silicone.

The Red Flags (Avoid These)

If you see these materials, put the toy down. They are porous, meaning they have microscopic holes that trap bacteria, dead skin cells, and fluids. They can cause infections, yeast imbalances, and bad odors.

  • Jelly Rubber / "Jelly" Toys: Often smell like chemicals. Highly porous.
  • TPR or TPE (unless specifically regulated): Sometimes used for realistic dildos or strokers. They require intense maintenance (boiling, powdering) and degrade over time.
  • Anything with Phthalates: Phthalates are chemicals used to make plastics soft. They are known endocrine disruptors.

Material Safety Checklist

  • Is it labeled 100% medical-grade or platinum-grade silicone?
  • Does it smell like strong plastic or chemicals out of the box? (If yes, return it).
  • Is the surface smooth without random sticky spots?

2. Beginner-Friendly Options by Category

If you're new to toys, start small and simple. You don't need a $200 app-controlled robot right out of the gate.

Vibrators

  • Bullet Vibrators: Tiny, discreet, and perfect for targeted clitoral stimulation. Great to hold during intercourse.
  • Wand Vibrators: Powerful, broad stimulation. The original Magic Wand is famous for a reason, but there are many smaller, rechargeable versions now.
  • Rabbit Vibrators: Designed for blended orgasms (internal G-spot stimulation + external clitoral stimulation simultaneously).
  • Air-Pulse / Suction Toys: These don't vibrate; they use gentle air pressure to simulate oral sex on the clitoris. Highly recommended for intense, quick orgasms.

Safety Checklist: Vibrators

  • Check if it is waterproof before submerging in the bath/shower.
  • Avoid using a vibrating toy for longer than 45-60 minutes to prevent numbing or desensitization.

Dildos and Penetrative Toys

A dildo is simply a non-vibrating toy used for penetration. For beginners, look for something with a slight curve (to target the G-spot or prostate) and a smooth silicone finish. Start with an average size—don't buy the biggest one you can find right away.

Safety Checklist: Dildos

  • Start slow and always use plenty of water-based lubricant to avoid micro-tears.
  • Never force insertion if you feel sharp pain—stop, breathe, and use a smaller size if necessary.

Couples Toys and Accessories

Toys designed to be worn during sex. The most common is the C-shaped vibrator: one end goes inside the vagina (stimulating the G-spot), while the other rests on the clitoris, vibrating for both partners during penetration.

Safety Checklist: Couples Toys

  • Communicate constantly: "Does this feel good? Is it pinching?"
  • Ensure the toy is comfortable for both partners during movement.

Anal Play Toys

Crucial Rule: Any toy going in the butt must have a flared base so it doesn't get lost inside. Look for small, beginner-friendly silicone butt plugs or anal beads. Start small, use plenty of lube, and go slow.

Safety Checklist: Anal Toys

  • Must have a flared base—the rectum creates a vacuum and will pull toys inside.
  • Never move a toy from the anus to the vagina without washing it thoroughly or changing the condom, to avoid bacterial infections.
  • Use a specialized, thick anal lubricant (water-based or silicone-based, depending on the toy material).

BDSM and Kink Gear

Curious about kink? Start with soft restraints (like silk ties or velcro cuffs), a lightweight riding crop, or a blindfold. Communication and a safe word are your most important tools here.

Safety Checklist: BDSM Gear

  • Always establish a safe word (e.g., "Red") before beginning any kink play.
  • Keep trauma shears nearby if using ropes or hard restraints, and monitor circulation (check for cold or blue fingers/toes).

Menstrual and Wellness Products

The wellness space now includes toys designed to relieve menstrual cramps (often using heat and vibration), pelvic floor trainers (Kegel weights), and specialized dilators for those experiencing pain during sex.

Lubes and Enhancers

Lube makes everything better. Seriously.

  • Water-Based Lube: Safe with all toys (including silicone) and condoms. Easy to clean. The best all-rounder.
  • Silicone-Based Lube: Extremely slippery, lasts forever, great for water play. Never use silicone lube with silicone toys—it will degrade the toy's surface.
  • Hybrid Lube: A mix of water and silicone.

3. The Practical Stuff: Size, Budget, Noise, and Accessibility

Accessibility Features for Different Needs

Pleasure is for everyone. If you have limited mobility, chronic pain, or neurodivergent sensory needs, here's what to look for:

  • Ergonomic Handles: Look for toys with large, looping handles or textured grips (like the LELO Oden or specific wands) that require less grip strength to hold.
  • App Control and Remotes: Many modern toys can be controlled via a smartphone app or a wireless remote, allowing you or a partner to adjust settings without reaching for tiny buttons on the toy itself.
  • Mountable Toys: Consider toys that can be mounted to a smooth surface (like a wall or shower tile) using suction cups, or specialized mounts and cushions (like Liberator pillows) that hold toys in place for hands-free use.
  • Sensory Considerations: If you are sensitive to noise or intense vibration, look for toys advertised as "low-frequency" or "rumbling" rather than "buzzy," and check decibel ratings in reviews.

Size Guides and Progression

Start with a size that matches what you're currently comfortable with. If you're buying a dildo, measure your favorite toy (or ask your partner) and match the girth (circumference) and insertable length. If you want to go bigger, progress slowly using training kits.

Budget Considerations (Budget vs. Premium)

You can get a fantastic, body-safe toy for $40–$60. Premium toys ($100+) usually offer better warranties, quieter motors, softer silicone, waterproofing, and tech features (like app control or interactive patterns). Don't buy a $10 jelly toy from a novelty shop—it's not worth the risk to your body.

Noise Levels and Discretion

If you have roommates or kids, noise matters. Bullet vibrators and smaller silicone toys are usually quieter. Wands tend to be louder. Look for reviews that specifically mention "whisper-quiet" motors.

4. Maintenance, Storage, and Warranties

Cleaning Protocols

Clean your toys before and after every use. Wash with warm water and unscented antibacterial soap, or a dedicated toy cleaner. Let them air dry completely before putting them away.

Storage and Discretion

Store silicone toys separately—if two silicone toys touch for a long time, they can actually melt together. Keep them in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Use a dedicated toy bag (often included with premium toys) or a clean, breathable cloth bag.

Warranty and Return Policies

Because of hygiene laws, you usually cannot return a sex toy once the seal is broken. However, reputable brands (like LELO, We-Vibe, Womanizer) offer 1-year to 10-year warranties on the motor and battery. Always register your toy if the manufacturer offers it.

5. Where to Shop

Where you buy matters as much as what you buy.

  • Online Retailers: Look for sex-positive, feminist, or wellness-focused online boutiques. They carefully curate their selections and usually only stock body-safe products.
  • In-Store: If you visit a brick-and-mortar shop, look for well-lit, clean boutiques that prioritize education. Don't be afraid to ask the staff questions—they are trained to help!
  • Avoid: Sketchy third-party sellers on giant marketplaces (like Amazon) where counterfeit toys are unfortunately common. A fake silicone toy might actually be cheap TPE mixed with chemicals.
