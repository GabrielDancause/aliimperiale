The Safety Guide: Materials & Maintenance

This is the boring stuff, but it is crucial. The toy industry is largely unregulated, meaning a lot of cheap, toxic crap gets sold in cute packaging.

Materials to ALWAYS Look For:

  • Medical-Grade / Platinum-Cured Silicone: The gold standard. It’s non-porous, boils safe (for sterilization), and retains heat well.
  • Borosilicate Glass: Hard, smooth, non-porous, and excellent for temperature play (warming in water or cooling in the fridge).
  • Stainless Steel or Aluminum: Heavy, durable, and non-porous.
  • ABS Plastic: Hard plastic often used for the base or handle of toys. It’s safe but usually doesn't feel as nice against sensitive skin.

Materials to AVOID (The "Toxic Toys"):

Avoid anything labeled as "Jelly," "Rubber," "PVC," "TPE/TPR" (unless it's a specific stroker toy that requires it, in which case use a condom), or toys that smell strongly of chemicals. These are porous materials. They absorb bacteria that cannot be washed out, and they often contain phthalates—chemicals added to soften plastics that are known endocrine disruptors.

Cleaning & Storage

  • Wash before and after every use: Use warm water and unscented, antibacterial soap. You can also use specialized toy cleaner, but soap and water are fine for silicone.
  • Dry completely: Let them air dry or pat them down with a lint-free cloth before storing.
  • Store separately: Silicone toys can literally melt into each other if they touch during storage. Keep them in individual breathable bags (cotton or satin, not plastic zip-locks).
  • Lube rules: Use water-based lube with silicone toys. Silicone-based lube will degrade silicone toys.

Shopping Tips: How to Choose

  • Start Small: You don't need a $200 app-controlled thrusting rabbit for your first toy. A simple bullet or small wand is a perfect entry point.
  • Consider Noise: If you live with roommates or family, check reviews for noise levels. Wands are notoriously loud, while many suction toys are very quiet.
  • Read the Dimensions: Pictures are deceiving. Always check the actual measurements (length and girth) before buying, especially for insertable toys.
  • Where to Buy: Avoid Amazon if possible—counterfeits are rampant. Buy directly from reputable brands (like Lelo, We-Vibe, Womanizer, Lovense) or trusted sex-positive boutiques (like Babeland, Good Vibrations, or Spectrum Boutique).

Budget Recommendations

  • Under $30: Stick to simple silicone bullets or small glass wands.
  • $50 - $100: The sweet spot for high-quality, rechargeable silicone vibrators and entry-level suction toys.
  • $150+: Premium toys with app connectivity, luxury materials, and advanced motor technology.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best sex toy for a beginner?
For most beginners, a small bullet vibrator or a simple, smooth external wand is highly recommended. They are unintimidating, easy to use, and focus on clitoral stimulation, which is key for many people. The magic lies in their simplicity and precise vibration.
How do I choose the right material for a sex toy?
Always look for 100% medical-grade silicone, borosilicate glass, or stainless steel. These materials are non-porous, meaning they won't harbor bacteria and are easy to clean. Avoid 'jelly', TPR, TPE, or PVC toys as they are porous and can leach harmful chemicals (phthalates).
How should I clean and store my sex toys?
Clean silicone, glass, or metal toys with warm water and unscented antibacterial soap, or a specialized toy cleaner. Dry completely before storing. Store toys separately in breathable bags (like cotton) or their original packaging. Never let silicone toys touch each other in storage, as they can melt together.
How do I introduce a sex toy to my partner?
Start the conversation outside the bedroom in a low-pressure environment. Frame it around enhancing mutual pleasure, not replacing them. You might say, 'I was reading about couples toys and thought it might be fun to explore together. What do you think?' Keep it curious and pressure-free.
Are expensive sex toys really better than cheap ones?
Generally, yes, up to a point. Higher price points usually mean body-safe materials (like medical-grade silicone), better motors (stronger, quieter vibrations), rechargeable batteries, and longer warranties. Very cheap toys often use porous, unsafe materials and break easily. Investing in a mid-range, body-safe toy is always better than buying multiple cheap, unsafe ones.
